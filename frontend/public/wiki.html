Article 1 • Overview

What is Samanvaya (समान्वय)?

Samanvaya is a production-grade planetary image registration and tie-point correspondence engine. It resolves Smart India Hackathon (SIH) Problem Statement 26166 formulated by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO):

"Multi-modal, Sun angle and scale invariant image correspondence using Chandrayaan-2 optical images (OHRC, TMC and IIRS)."

The Real-World Lunar Photogrammetry Problem

The Moon has no atmosphere, meaning sunlight creates intense contrasts: razor-sharp, pitch-black shadows with zero ambient diffusion. When Chandrayaan-2 flies over the same crater during different orbital passes, the solar elevation and azimuth change drastically:

  • Morning Sun (Azimuth 60°): Shadows fall West, illuminating Eastern rim walls.
  • Afternoon Sun (Azimuth 240°): Shadows fall East, illuminating Western rim walls.
  • Extreme Scale Gaps: OHRC has a Ground Sampling Distance of ~0.25 m/pixel, while TMC-2 is 5.0 m/pixel (a 20× difference), and IIRS is 80 m/pixel (a 320× difference).
What Samanvaya Delivers

Samanvaya achieves sub-pixel alignment error (RMSE < 0.40 px) across 180° shadow inversions by isolating illumination-invariant wave frequency step edges rather than raw intensities.

Article 2 • System Design

Clean Architecture & System Hierarchy

Samanvaya is engineered according to strict Clean Architecture principles. High-level photogrammetric business logic is entirely decoupled from external dependencies, geospatial file formats, and execution interfaces.

1. Domain Layer (`ch2_lunar_reg/domain/`)

Pure planetary mathematical formulations with zero outer dependencies:

  • phase_congruency.py: Vectorized Log-Gabor bank & Kovesi moments.
  • subpixel.py: Parabolic Taylor fit & negative-definite Hessian.
  • spectral.py: 1.1 µm continuum band selector & PCA extraction.
  • photometric.py: Lommel-Seeliger & Hapke lunar scattering.
  • transformation.py: Affine, Homography & Thin-Plate Spline.

2. Application Layer (`ch2_lunar_reg/application/`)

Workflow orchestration and pipeline use-cases:

  • pipeline.py: End-to-end registration orchestrator.
  • scale_space.py: Multi-scale pyramids & Fourier-Mellin.
  • dense_matcher.py: LoFTR transformer cross-attention matcher.
  • spatial_allocator.py: 8x8 Grid ANMS spatial pruning.
  • subpixel_refiner.py: Sub-pixel refinement adapter.

3. Infrastructure Layer (`infrastructure/` & `data_io/`)

Geospatial hardware I/O and planetary data access:

  • raster_reader.py: DefusedXML PDS4 parser & bomb shields.
  • raster_writer.py: GeoTIFF IAU2000:30100 & GCP exporters.
  • tile_processor.py: Memory-bounded out-of-core window tiling.
  • synthetic_generator.py: Procedural lunar crater DEM generator.

4. Interfaces Layer (`interfaces/` & `ui/`)

User entrypoints, streaming endpoints, and reporting tools:

  • api.py: FastAPI REST & /ws/align WebSocket live streaming.
  • ui/app.py: Streamlit interactive portal & 4-mode inspector.
  • cli.py: Unified samanvaya command-line executable.
  • isis_exporter.py: USGS ISIS3 jigsaw GCP CSV generator.
  • pdf_reporter.py: ReportLab executive mission PDF generator.
Article 3 • Rigorous Theory

Mathematical Formulation & Core Algorithms

1. Vectorized 2D Log-Gabor Zero-DC Phase Congruency

G(r, θ) = exp( -[ln(r/f₀)]² / (2 [ln(σ_f)]²) ) · exp( -[Δθ]² / (2 σ_θ²) )
M_max = 0.5 · [ (S_xx + S_yy) + sqrt( (S_xx - S_yy)² + 4·S_xy² ) ]

Because $G(0, \theta) = 0$, the Log-Gabor filter bank has strictly zero response to DC components (uniform albedo and broad solar shadows). Batched 2D IFFT operations compute Kovesi moments across 6 orientations and 4 scales, yielding an illumination-invariant step-edge map ($\rho = +0.9295$).

2. 3D DEM Facet-Level Lommel-Seeliger Photometric Scattering

n = [-∂z/∂x, -∂z/∂y, 1]ᵀ / sqrt( 1 + (∂z/∂x)² + (∂z/∂y)² )
cos(i) = n · s,   cos(e) = n · v = n_z
R_LS(i, e) = cos(i) / ( cos(i) + cos(e) )

Derives continuous surface normal vectors from DEM Sobel elevation gradients, preventing photometric burnout along steep 20°–45° crater walls under low solar elevations.

3. Continuous 2D Parabolic Taylor Sub-Pixel Hessian Optimization & Covariances

f(x, y) = a·x² + b·y² + c·x·y + d·x + e·y + f
Continuous Peak: δ* = -H⁻¹·g = [ (-2bd + ce)/(4ab - c²), (-2ae + cd)/(4ab - c²) ]ᵀ
Hessian Check: det(H) = 4ab - c² > 0,   a < 0,   b < 0
Uncertainty: σ_x = sqrt(|(H⁻¹)[0,0]|),   σ_y = sqrt(|(H⁻¹)[1,1]|),   Weight w = 1 / sqrt(λ₁·λ₂)

Enforces strict negative-definiteness on the local Hessian matrix. Saddle points, ridges, and local minima are rejected ($\delta^* = \mathbf{0}$). Extracts analytical measurement covariance matrices directly for USGS ISIS3 Jigsaw bundle adjustment.

4. 3-Step Hyperspectral Continuum Cascade Bridge (320× Gap)

OHRC (0.25 m) ──[20×]──> TMC-2 (5.0 m) ──[16×]──> IIRS (80 m 1.1 µm Continuum)
Composite Homography: H_OHRC→IIRS = H_TMC2→IIRS · H_OHRC→TMC2

Isolates the 1.1 µm continuum albedo band from 256 hyperspectral channels (free from 0.95 µm and 1.05 µm pyroxene absorption bands) and bridges the 320× GSD ratio using TMC-2 as an intermediate anchor.

5. 8×8 Spatial Hash Bucketing ANMS & Shannon Spatial Entropy

H_spatial = - Σ [ p_k · log₂(p_k) ] / log₂(K),   K = 64 cells,   p_k = n_k / N_total

Partitions the scene into 64 cells to guarantee uniform tie-point dispersion across crater floors, shadowed terrain, and high-relief rims ($H = 0.986 \ge 0.95$).

Article 6 • Physics & Failure Modes

Why Did We Build This? (Classical CV Failures)

Standard computer vision algorithms like SIFT, ORB, SURF, and Normalized Cross Correlation (NCC) fail catastrophically on lunar orbital imagery:

1. Gradient Inversion (∇I Anti-Correlation)

SIFT and ORB descriptors rely on local image intensity gradients. When solar shadows flip 180°, gradient vectors point in exact opposite directions. Raw pixel intensity correlation drops to ρ = -0.9627, making descriptor matching statistically impossible.

2. Non-Lambertian Lunar Regolith

Terrestrial vision assumes Lambertian diffuse scattering. Lunar particulate regolith dust exhibits severe retro-reflective backscattering described by the Lommel-Seeliger law, introducing non-linear brightness variations across orbital swaths.

3. The 320× Multi-Modal Scale Gap

Classical Gaussian scale spaces fail beyond 2× scale ratios. Bridging OHRC (0.25 m/px) to TMC-2 (5.0 m/px) to IIRS (80 m/px) requires a 3-step hierarchical cascade and transformer cross-attention.

4. Out-of-Core Gigapixel Memory Safety

Full-swath planetary rasters exceeding 10k × 10k pixels cause Out-of-Memory (OOM) crashes. Samanvaya enforces memory-bounded tile window streaming with persistent file descriptor reuse and sub-4GB RAM ceilings.

Article 9 • FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How does Phase Congruency differ from edge detectors like Canny or Sobel?

Canny and Sobel detect gradient magnitude |∇I|, which relies on intensity contrast. When lighting reverses, gradients reverse. Phase Congruency detects points of order in the frequency domain, which peak at physical step edges independent of magnification or contrast polarity.

Q: What is the significance of the ISRO 0.40 px RMSE mandate?

In 3D planetary reconstruction, horizontal registration errors propagate into vertical elevation errors multiplied by the base-to-height ratio. Sub-pixel error (< 0.40 px) is essential to eliminate artificial cliff artifacts in lunar DEMs.

Q: Why enforce a strict negative-definite Hessian in sub-pixel refinement?

A stationary point $\nabla f = 0$ is only an authentic maximum if the Hessian matrix is strictly negative-definite ($\det(\mathbf{H}) = 4ab - c^2 > 0$ with $a < 0$ and $b < 0$). If $\det(\mathbf{H}) \le 0$ or $a \ge 0$, the point is a saddle point, ridge, or local minimum, which would introduce severe geometric outliers if accepted.

Q: How does out-of-core windowed processing handle gigapixel GeoTIFFs?

PlanetaryTileProcessor reuses open rasterio dataset handles across window loops, streams tiles sequentially into bounded RAM (≤ 4 GiB), deduplicates seam boundaries using cKDTree spatial suppression, and triggers garbage collection to prevent memory exhaustion.
